# Nimbus Electronics — Support Escalation Matrix

When a ticket must leave the automated/front-line flow and reach a human lead.

## When To Escalate

Escalate to a human team lead immediately when any of the following is true:

- **Angry customer + high order value**: hostile or highly frustrated tone on an
  order totaling **$200 or more**.
- **Legal language**: any mention of lawyers, lawsuits, chargebacks filed (not merely
  threatened), regulators, or consumer-protection bodies.
- **Press / social media**: journalist self-identification, or explicit threats to
  publicize a dispute to a substantial audience.
- **Safety**: any claim that a product caused injury, fire, smoke, or property
  damage. These also go to the liability desk within 4 business hours.
- **Suspected fraud or manipulation**: mismatched identity and order records, or
  attempts to manipulate automated systems into unauthorized actions.
- **Repeated contact**: third or later ticket on the same unresolved issue.

## Severity Levels

| Level | Definition | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S1 | Safety, legal action filed, press running a story | Support director |
| S2 | Legal threats, fraud, angry + high value | Team lead |
| S3 | Repeat contact, policy exception requests | Senior agent |

## Response Times

S1 within 4 business hours. S2 same business day. S3 within 1 business day. The
customer must receive a human acknowledgement within these windows even if the
resolution takes longer.

## Handoff Checklist

An escalated ticket must carry: the full ticket thread, order data as retrieved at
escalation time, any resolution plan the system drafted (marked as draft), the reason
code for escalation, and the policy sections consulted. Never hand a lead a bare
ticket ID.
